The Bamako Convoy

Sun Tzu on the supply-line as the target ahead of the enemy’s army.

Sun Tzu’s discipline in the second chapter names the specifically-material principle that the campaign turns on provisions, not on battle: an army without grain is lost, an army without transport is lost, an army without stores is lost. JNIM has read the chapter directly. The group has not attempted to storm Bamako — the April 2026 offensive briefly contested the perimeter and immediately withdrew — and it does not need to. What it has done is convert the two road corridors on which the capital depends into a specifically-material kill-box: the RN6 to Côte d’Ivoire and the RN1 to Senegal are single-lane, largely un-paved for long stretches, run through open bush that the group has scouted for eighteen months, and cross bridges the group can pre-mine at leisure. Each tanker destroyed is a two-hundred-thousand-euro capital loss the junta covers, a five-day gap in Bamako’s diesel inventory, and a specifically-material recruitment poster in the villages the group operates from. The Sun Tzu reading is that JNIM has understood that in a landlocked polity the supply-line is the centre of gravity and the capital is the trailing indicator; the group has attacked the centre of gravity while leaving the capital as a permanent hostage, and every fortnight the blockade holds is a fortnight the junta pays for in the currency the group is actually collecting — the specifically-material legitimacy of the state as the provider of the fuel that runs the taxi, the generator, and the hospital.

Clausewitz on the centre of gravity that is not the capital but the road that feeds it.

Clausewitz’s eighth book names the Schwerpunkt — the centre of gravity, the specifically-material point at which the enemy’s cohesion depends and against which the campaign must be concentrated. The junta’s pre-blockade doctrine, inherited from the French Barkhane counter-insurgency template, treated the centre of gravity as territorial: hold the cities, hold the airport, hold the northern garrison towns of Kidal and Gao and pursue the mobile enemy in the bush. JNIM has inverted the frame. The specifically-material Schwerpunkt of the modern Malian state is neither Bamako nor its garrisons but the two-corridor road grid through which its energy imports arrive. Every ministry, every hospital, every telecommunications relay, every police barracks, every fuel-fired power station runs off the same diesel. The group’s three-hundred-twenty destroyed tankers are not attritional targets — each is a Clausewitzian strike at the centre of gravity, delivered at a cost the state cannot match and at a tempo the state cannot answer. The Clausewitz reading is that a state whose energy imports run through a single-lane road three hundred kilometres inside insurgent operating range has published its Schwerpunkt in advance; the specifically-material campaign is not the pursuit of the enemy inside the bush but the defence of the road the state itself is on.

Greene’s Law 3 on the intention JNIM has instead publicly announced to compound the coercive effect.

Greene’s Third Law — Conceal Your Intentions — is the tactical default of the operator whose specifically-material problem is that the target will pre-empt the move if it is signalled. JNIM has inverted the law. Al-Bambari’s September 2025 video announcement of the blockade, the group’s WhatsApp channel to the transport unions naming the axes it will interdict, the specifically-material public naming of Guinea and Senegal as the neighbours whose road-corridor operators will be attacked if they run the blockade — all of this is broadcast intention, not concealed. The inversion is deliberate. In a specifically-material siege, the coercive payload is not the fuel physically destroyed but the fuel that fails to be shipped because the transport company has priced the ambush risk into its refusal to run. Every publicly-announced ambush against a Bougouni convoy adds three days to the next tanker’s pre-shipment insurance negotiation in Abidjan; every named neighbour learns that its road operators will be attacked if they run the blockade. The Greene reading is that the group has read Law 3 and calibrated against it: the intention is announced because the announcement is the weapon, and the concealment discipline of the traditional insurgent is a category error against a supply-chain target whose participants are civilians who can read the news.

Lee Kuan Yew on the small polity whose fragility is the single supply-chain that runs through the neighbours.

Lee Kuan Yew’s specifically-material discipline on the Singaporean predicament — a small polity dependent on water from Johor, oil from the Middle East transiting Malacca, and food from Malaysia — was that the sovereignty of the state is exactly co-extensive with the diversification of its supply chain, and that any polity whose supply of one strategic input runs through one neighbour is that neighbour’s vassal in a crisis. The specifically-material Malian problem is worse. Ninety-five per cent of diesel and petrol enter through two corridors, both of which cross open bush inside insurgent operating range, both of which terminate in the ports of two neighbours (Abidjan, Dakar) whose ECOWAS relations with Bamako collapsed in 2024, both of which are single points of failure the insurgent has read as inputs into the campaign. Reading Note

The lenses used.

What’s driving this. Two layers carry the work. Structural: the specific mechanism is that Mali is landlocked, imports ninety-five per cent of its diesel and petrol by road, and depends on two single-lane corridors (RN6 to Côte d’Ivoire, RN1 to Senegal) that cross three-hundred-kilometre stretches of open bush inside insurgent operating range — the geography of the supply chain is what makes the blockade cheap for JNIM and expensive for the state, and no amount of Africa Corps deployment closes the mobility gap. What’s not driving this. Not any specifically-material JNIM territorial ambition on Bamako itself — the group has not attempted to hold the capital and does not have the manpower or structure to do so; the reader who codes the blockade as a preliminary to a JNIM assault on the presidential palace has mistaken the doctrine. The specifically-material driver is the supply-chain-as-centre-of-gravity, not the capital-as-centre-of-gravity.

  • A JNIM-Bamako negotiated fuel-corridor deal inside the fortnight — that would mean the group has moved from siege to a taxation-of-the-corridor posture (levying tolls on tankers rather than destroying them), the doctrine has evolved into a proto-governance instrument, and the reading resolves as an economic-warfare campaign successfully converted into political rents.
