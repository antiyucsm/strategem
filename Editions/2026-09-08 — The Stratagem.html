The Ottawa Retort

At one minute past midnight on Tuesday 8 September, Canada’s Prime Minister Mark Carney switched on a retaliatory tariff schedule of twenty-five per cent on roughly twenty billion Canadian dollars of US-origin steel, aluminium alloy, dairy, poultry, and consumer electronics, and stripped selected US firms of their status inside the CUSMA rules-of-origin architecture. The move answers a Trump-White-House decision two weeks earlier to lift the Section-232 steel-and-aluminium duty on Canada to fifty per cent inside a broader Section-232 escalation whose Canada-specific line item Washington had held for August. Carney’s Liberal minority government has spent August briefing that a proportional Canadian answer would arrive on the anniversary of the September 2018 CUSMA signature, and that the schedule would target Republican-state export lines — Pennsylvania steel, Wisconsin dairy, Ohio poultry, California table wine, Texas petrochemical intermediates — whose Senate delegations sit between the White House and the Q4 defence-authorisation calendar. Read as a domestic-audience Ottawa gesture, or as a trade-desk retaliation of the standard form, the Retort absorbs. Read as statecraft it is the moment at which the second-oldest bilateral trade relationship in the OECD ledger prices its own dissolution against a US administration whose extraction cycle has moved from allies-outside-the-tent to allies-of-record; at which the CUSMA July-2026 six-year review clock accelerates from mandatory to terminal; and at which a middle-power state that had spent eighty years managing its dependence on the American consumer discovers the discipline of pricing the dependence back at the counterparty’s Senate delegation.

Talleyrand on the retaliation that names the counterparty’s political heartland.

The Talleyrand principle in bilateral diplomacy is that a punitive schedule is meaningless unless the pain it delivers is legible at the specific electoral geography the counterparty draws his authority from. Carney’s tariff schedule is not the drafting of a Global Affairs desk officer running the WTO retaliation calculus; it is a Prime Minister’s Office document whose line items track Republican-vulnerable House and Senate delegations district-by-district. Pennsylvania steel exports the schedule’s twenty-five-per-cent duty against a Cleveland-Cliffs Butler-Works plant whose margins print in Bob Casey’s successor seat; Wisconsin dairy sits inside Ron Johnson’s Foxconn-adjacent constituency whose 2026 mid-term is priced against the same milk-price line; Ohio poultry runs through Tim Ryan-successor turf whose Trumbull-Mahoning voter base receives the tariff via the price of a Kroger drumstick; the California table-wine schedule prices Napa-Sonoma against a Democratic-Senate arithmetic the White House will hear about first from its own donor calls; the Texas petrochemical intermediates line runs the tariff through Corpus Christi-Beaumont into the industrial-corridor mid-term math John Cornyn is defending. The Talleyrand reading is that Ottawa has named, on the schedule’s printed line, the specifically-material political counties inside which the White House’s tariff will be paid, and has done so at a Senate arithmetic Chuck Grassley cannot ignore inside the Q4 defence-authorisation cycle. The counterparty was told, in the currency he understands, which of his own precincts will spend the extraction.

Clausewitz on the tariff war that continues by other means.

Clausewitz’s working thesis is that war is the continuation of politics by other means; its lesser cousin is that a tariff war is the continuation of a political-economy negotiation by the means available when the diplomatic architecture has collapsed. What collapsed here is not CUSMA’s legal text but its political premise — that Canada would absorb a proportional US escalation at every cycle in exchange for continued deep integration inside the North-American industrial base. The Trump-administration Section-232 posture has been that the premise is renegotiable in the counterparty’s favour; Carney’s 8 September schedule is the specifically-material demonstration that Ottawa has read the premise as terminal and has moved to price its own exit. The Clausewitz reading is that Canada is now fighting a limited economic war for the specifically-material objective of restoring the pre-2025 political-economy premise, and that Ottawa’s war aims are calibrated: no CUSMA suspension, no C$-oil-embargo (which would be the escalation to unlimited war), no cross-border rail interruption. What Carney has authorised is a schedule whose damage arrives at the counterparty’s political price-of-continuation, not at the counterparty’s industrial-base survival. The war is winnable at Ottawa’s terms because it is limited at Ottawa’s ambition.

Mearsheimer on the middle-power that runs out of hedges.

Offensive realism’s reading of a middle-power inside an alliance guarantor’s security architecture is that the middle-power hedges by paying tribute to the guarantor at the schedule the guarantor sets, and that the tribute is priced against a continued extension of the security umbrella that would otherwise be unaffordable. The specifically-material problem Ottawa faced through 2025 and August 2026 is that the security umbrella’s price has risen — NORAD contribution formulas, F-35 penalty clauses, Arctic-corridor Coast-Guard obligations, the ongoing Trump-himself threat to name Canada as an economic annexation target on a Truth Social morning — while the counterparty’s extraction cycle has been priced up on the same calendar. The Mearsheimer reading is that Carney’s Liberal cabinet has judged that the marginal tribute the umbrella demands now exceeds the marginal deterrence the umbrella supplies, and has begun to price the alternative: an EU trade-and-defence relationship deepened by the September CETA-plus talks, a UK bilateral running through a Starmer-Carney Downing Street channel that had already priced the Canadian Rooivalk-plus and Type-26 frigate calendars against non-US suppliers, and a Pacific-corridor South Korea and Japan bilateral whose critical-minerals off-take architecture the Retort’s electronics line quietly opens. Ottawa is not defecting from the alliance; it is pricing the defection option publicly, so that the next tribute demand arrives inside a counterparty calculation that includes it.

La Rochefoucauld on self-interest that speaks the language of principle.

La Rochefoucauld’s working maxim is that self-interest speaks all languages, including the language of disinterestedness, and the discipline of reading a public statement is to find the interest the language is drafted to conceal. The Carney press release names “proportionality,” “rules-based order,” and “shared prosperity” on the front paragraph; the interest the language is drafted to conceal is a Liberal minority government that lost thirteen points of vote share to the Conservatives in the March by-election cycle, that faces a Bloc Québécois confidence threshold every ten weeks, and whose only remaining electoral instrument is a nationalist trade posture the population had been demanding since the January 2025 Trump inauguration. The Rochefoucauld reading is that the Retort is domestically over-determined and diplomatically calibrated: Carney had to sign this schedule to hold the government together through the October Ontario provincial calendar, and the schedule’s specific composition was drafted by a PMO that read the Senate arithmetic more carefully than the Global Affairs briefing memo did. The reader who takes the “rules-based order” language at face value has mistaken the instrument. The reader who prices the schedule at the intersection of the Bloc confidence-threshold calendar and the Cornyn-Grassley Senate math is watching the negotiation the language is drafted to conceal.

Which of your own retaliations are drafted at the counterparty’s political heartland rather than at the procedural forum, which of your August covers has just been cancelled by a September datum you now must publicly reverse, and which of your inevitable defeats can still be re-timed and re-framed so the loss serves your successor rather than the counterparty who inflicts it?
