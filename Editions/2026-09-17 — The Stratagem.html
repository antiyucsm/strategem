Mearsheimer on the institution the hegemon no longer arbitrates.

The offensive-realist working discipline is that international institutions run when a leading power has the standing capability and the standing interest to enforce their output, and lapse into ritual when either is missing. The 1737 Committee is a limit case. The United States has the capability and the appetite — it is at war with the sanctions’ target — but it does not have Council standing behind it: Washington abandoned the JCPOA in 2018, and the E3 rather than the P5 is running the machine. That inversion is what today’s vote exposes. When the Council’s Iran file is administered by three European foreign ministries against the standing objection of two permanent members, and the fourth permanent member is running its own bilateral war, the arbitration function has already moved. Mearsheimer’s specifically-material read is not that the sanctions collapse; it is that the venue collapses. Every subsequent Iran move — a maritime interdiction, a listings vote, a designations expansion — will be made in London, Paris, Berlin or Washington and defended at Turtle Bay only for the record, and the record is what Russia and China are auditioning to render worthless. The specifically-material tell is whether the E3 tables new listings under national law by end-October or defers them into the December EU Council — the first is a signal the machine has moved to Brussels, the second is a signal Turtle Bay is being kept alive as a fiction.

Sun Tzu on attacking the coalition rather than the army.

Chapter III of the Art of War ranks the attack on the enemy’s alliances above the attack on the enemy’s army, because the alliance cost is compounding and the battlefield cost is spent. Russia and China are not attacking the sanctions today; they are attacking the register at which the E3 speaks for the international community. If Nebenzia and Fu Cong can hold the position that the Council “cannot proceed” on the 1737 file, then every listings action after today is a Western action rather than a UN action, and every non-aligned state — the Gulf, the AU rotation, India, Brazil — is handed a defensible reason to under-comply. The Iranian foreign ministry has been briefing precisely this register through the summer, and the Baqaei press conferences framing the Council as “divided” are the coordinated content layer of the same move. The specifically-material Sun Tzu payload is not that Iran wins the vote — it will not — but that the Council’s political product is decoupled from the Council’s legal product. The West holds the paper; the multipolar coalition holds the readership. That is the second-order defeat Chapter III names.

Talleyrand on the treaty that survives its instrument.

Charles-Maurice de Talleyrand’s working discipline through Vienna, London and Frankfurt was that treaties outlive their signatories only when the daily instrument set the treaty produces (a committee, an inspector, a listings desk) has a domestic clientele in every capital that will fight for its budget line. The JCPOA never developed one. The IAEA safeguards apparatus did, and that is the reason the specifically-material NPT-safeguards track remains funded and staffed after the political treaty collapsed. The 1737 Committee falls between: it has a legal foundation the E3 has now restored, and no domestic clientele in Moscow or Beijing that will defend its Panel of Experts’ travel budget. Today’s vote is Talleyrand’s specifically-material test: does the E3 have enough leverage over the UN Secretariat’s administrative machinery (procurement, translation, security, per-diem) to keep the Panel operational even without a consensus mandate? The Secretariat’s public silence for a fortnight suggests the answer is provisionally yes, but the durability of that yes is set by whether the P5 rotation can slow-roll the Committee’s working papers, and by whether the Non-Aligned Movement members currently on the Council (Guyana, Sierra Leone, Slovenia in the West-Europe seat) accept a Chair role or refuse it. Ambassador rotation on the Committee itself is not a ceremonial question. It is the specifically-material budget-line vote.

Greene’s Law 20 on refusing the seat rather than losing the vote.

Robert Greene’s Twentieth Law — Do Not Commit to Anyone — is the operator’s discipline of keeping option value by declining to be booked into the counterparty’s frame. Russia and China are not fighting to win today’s procedural vote; they are fighting to keep from being counted inside it. Their preferred outcome is not 8-7 in their favour, which they cannot deliver, but 11-2-2 again — the same coalition, refusing again, on the record again, with the Committee’s legal survival demonstrated to be dependent on a nine-vote Western coalition that no non-aligned state actively joined. The Western vote count is the metric the Council uses; the abstention count is the metric the Non-Aligned Movement uses. If two abstentions become four this afternoon — Pakistan and Somalia holding, plus a Guyana or a Sierra Leone drifting — the specifically-material Law 20 gain is not a defeat handed to the E3; it is a widening of the coalition of Council members who will not sign either side’s paper, which is exactly the ambiguity register the Iranian negotiating position depends on. Watch the abstention count, not the yes-vote count.

Reading Note

The lenses used.

What’s driving this. Two layers carry the work. Coordination: the specific mechanism is that two permanent members of the Council are running a year-long, publicly-scripted refusal to acknowledge the E3-restored sanctions regime as legally valid — Moscow and Beijing coordinate the floor speeches, the abstention-count target, the Iranian press-conference amplification, and the corridor briefings to non-aligned delegations that make abstaining the reputationally-cheap move. This is not spontaneous divergence; it is a written joint position with two matching foreign-ministry legal opinions dated to October 2025, which is the definition of coordinated elite action across institutions. Structural: the specific mechanism is that the Council’s Iran arbitration function requires a hegemon prepared to expend agenda-setting capital to defend it, and no such hegemon is present — the US is running a bilateral war on its own track, the E3 has the legal position but not the enforcement platform, and the Council’s residual arbitration authority is decaying at the exact rate at which the machine’s day-to-day outputs (listings, Panel reports, referrals) can be substituted by EU-27 and national instruments.

What’s not driving this. Not Iranian diplomacy at the venue. Tehran is not represented on the Council, has no vote, and its posture is downstream of Moscow and Beijing’s. The reader who codes the Refusal as an Iranian success has mistaken the amplifier for the amp; the vote is being fought between five permanent members and ten elected ones, and Iran’s role is to convert the outcome into content, not to shape it.

What would break this reading.

  • A Russian or Chinese veto attempt on any E3 draft this afternoon — today’s Committee vote is procedural, not subject to veto, but a veto attempt on a substantive text would move the reading from “institution decaying quietly” to “institution actively fractured” and force the E3 to move listings to Brussels inside the week.
  • A specifically-material US absence, abstention, or dissenting vote — the Trump administration has kept Iran policy on a bilateral track, and an American refusal to line up with the E3 today would score the fissure as three-way, not two-way, and would tell every non-aligned capital that the transatlantic consensus is ambient rather than real.
  • An Iranian NPT-withdrawal notification inside the fortnight — this would ratify the “sanctions are void” framing at the safeguards register that even Russia and China have so far declined to touch, and would force the E3 to choose between escalation to a Chapter VII referral (which Moscow would veto) and de-facto acceptance that the multilateral file is closed.
  • A fresh E3 fine-and-listings package on a Chinese refiner buying Iranian crude, or a bank clearing IRGC-linked payments — this would demonstrate the E3 has moved the machine to Brussels-and-London-and-Berlin without waiting for December, and would score the reading’s central claim (that the venue has already migrated) as under-priced rather than premature.
